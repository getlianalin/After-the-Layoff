Companies without a public program — but worth calling

These are ordinary customer service lines, not government or officially-published assistance programs. Some companies (like Sallie Mae below) do have a documented hardship process; most don't publish one at all, but will still work something out if you ask. Treat every number here as worth a try, never a guarantee — and always ask specifically for a "hardship," "financial assistance," or "payment plan" option by name.

Not sure where to start? Call 211 from any phone — a live person will help you figure out what applies to you.
This page links to official government, nonprofit, and utility company programs. It doesn't collect any personal information, and it isn't a government or utility website itself. Eligibility rules and application steps change — always confirm details on the provider's own site.
Many programs here are based on household income, not just your own — if you live with a spouse, partner, or family, their income usually counts too, even if you're the one who lost a job. Limits also vary by household size, so it's worth applying even if you're not sure you'll qualify.
Utility company discounts (gas, electric, water, internet, phone) depend on which specific company serves your address. This can vary block by block, and water service especially: some cities have four or five different water companies each covering different streets. Check a recent bill for your provider's exact name before applying — don't assume based on the city alone.
Only a few states have detailed local listings so far (California, Texas, New York, Florida), and utility-company programs are currently listed for the Los Angeles and San Diego areas only. The national and statewide programs above apply more broadly, and 211 can point you to local help anywhere.
